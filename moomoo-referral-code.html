Moomoo fees, honestly

Moomoo's pricing is unusual: excellent in one direction, mediocre in the other. Which one matters depends entirely on what you buy.

  • US stocks and ETFs: $0 commission. Genuinely free, which is the platform's strongest selling point.
  • Canadian stocks and ETFs: about $1.50 per trade. Not expensive, but not free either — and Wealthsimple charges nothing for the same trade.
  • Currency conversion: roughly 0.55% when converting CAD to USD. This is the fee to watch, because it applies every time you move money into US stocks.
  • Options: $0 base commission per contract, with regulatory fees of roughly $0.65 per contract still applying.
  • No account minimum and no inactivity fee.

Worth being precise about one thing: Moomoo is sometimes described as having no currency conversion cost. That is not accurate — the roughly 0.55% spread is real, and on a $10,000 conversion it is about $55. It is still competitive, since the big banks charge considerably more, but it is not zero.

Is Moomoo safe?

Moomoo Financial Canada is registered with the Canadian Investment Regulatory Organization and is a member of the Canadian Investor Protection Fund, with eligible accounts covered up to $1 million. The parent company, Futu Holdings, is listed on the Nasdaq.

CIPF coverage protects your assets if the broker itself fails. It does not protect you from losing money on your trades, and the free Level 2 data and extended hours access do not make active trading a reliably profitable activity — most people who try it underperform a plain index fund.

Tax on the bonus

The CRA generally treats a referral or signup bonus as income in the year you receive it, and any later gain when you sell as a capital gain, where half is taxable. On a bonus of this size the tax is trivial, but the record-keeping habit matters once real sums are involved. Keep your transaction history and talk to an accountant — this page is not tax advice.
