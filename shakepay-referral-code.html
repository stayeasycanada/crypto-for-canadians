The short verdict

Get Shakepay if this is your first time buying crypto, you want Bitcoin or Ethereum and nothing else, and you would rather pay a little more than learn a trading interface.

Skip it if you plan to buy regularly or in larger amounts. The spread quietly costs far more than NDAX's 0.2% over time, and it is invisible because it is baked into the price you are quoted.

Shakepay fees, honestly

Shakepay advertises no trading commission, which is true and also slightly misleading. The company makes its money on the spread — the gap between the market price of Bitcoin and the price it quotes you.

  • Trading: no commission, but a spread of up to roughly 3% on both buys and sells, depending on market conditions.
  • CAD deposits and withdrawals: free by Interac e-Transfer. This is genuinely free and unusual.
  • Crypto withdrawals: network fees only, which vary with how congested the network is.
  • Assets: Bitcoin and Ethereum only. No other coins, which is a feature if you were never going to buy them and a dealbreaker if you were.

Put that in dollars: on a $1,000 purchase a 3% spread costs you about $30 — which happens to be exactly your welcome bonus. On NDAX the same purchase costs about $2. That does not make Shakepay a bad choice, but it does mean the bonus is roughly a one-time refund of your first big trade, not free money on top.

ShakingSats and the Shakepay Card

  • ShakingSats gives you a tiny daily amount of Bitcoin for shaking your phone. The amounts are genuinely small — think cents, not dollars — so treat it as a habit-builder rather than income.
  • The Shakepay Card is a Visa card that pays bitcoin cashback on everyday spending. It is the most interesting thing Shakepay offers, because it accumulates Bitcoin without you deciding to buy any.

Is Shakepay safe?

Shakepay is registered with FINTRAC and with Canadian securities regulators, has operated since 2015, and holds the majority of crypto in cold storage. It has not had a publicly reported breach.

Two caveats that apply to every platform on this site. Crypto held on an exchange is not covered by CDIC insurance the way bank deposits are. And the platform being safe says nothing about the asset — Bitcoin has lost more than half its value within a single year more than once.

Tax on the bonus

The CRA generally treats a referral or signup bonus as income in the year you receive it, and any later gain when you sell as a capital gain, where half is taxable. On a bonus of this size the tax is trivial, but the record-keeping habit matters once real sums are involved. Keep your transaction history and talk to an accountant — this page is not tax advice.
